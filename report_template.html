    Run–walk

    These are Apple Watch runs that repeat a run piece and a walk. A session is listed when the same pair happens at least three times. The pairs are 4 minutes running and 1 minute walking, 9 minutes and 1 minute, and 12 minutes and 3 minutes. Short strides are not in this section.

    Distance is the length of a run piece. The minutes do not change, so a longer piece is a faster piece. Recovery is the average heart rate of the run piece minus the lowest heart rate on the walk after it. A higher number means the heart fell further during the walk. The lowest beat is the minimum the watch stored for that step.

    These runs stay out of the steady-run charts. Decoupling describes a steady run, not intervals (C3).

    Pace of the run pieces

    Each point is the mean pace of the run pieces in one session. Faster pace is at the top.

    Recovery on the walks

    Each point is the mean recovery of the walks in one session, in beats per minute.

    Distance of the run pieces

    Each point is the mean distance of the run pieces in one session of the pattern above.

    Distance against recovery

    Each point is one run piece and the walk after it. Distance is on the horizontal axis. Recovery is on the vertical axis.

    Best times

    A recorded best is the fastest average pace over a whole workout that covers the distance, up to twice as long. The time is that pace times the distance. A run that covers the distance at that average pace holds a stretch at least that fast. Indoor runs are out. Races from the journal count too. Outdoor GPS distance can be wrong by about 4 to 6 percent.

    The equivalent column is the best 5 km scaled with the Riegel exponent 1.06. It is a model, not a result.

    Trend across time

    Each point is one Apple Watch run. Blue is . Green is . Faster pace is at the top of the pace chart. Run–walk intervals are in their own section, not in these charts (C3). Runs from other apps count in the hour totals only when the Watch did not record the same run (M1).

    Heart rate against output

    Each point is the mean for one run. Apple checks a 5-second heart-rate sample against a chest strap. A run mean moves less than one sample. Apple does not give a watt error for running power. Use the watts to compare this watch with itself.

    Efficiency factor, split by heart-rate band

    Efficiency factor is mean power divided by mean heart rate. The unit is watts per beat. Couzens compares it only between similar efforts (C4). A mixed line across bands shows a change of pace, not of fitness. Compare points in one band, and keep heat in mind (C5).

    Aerobic decoupling

    Couzens uses the Joe Friel split (C1). Divide the run into two equal times. Use only samples while you move at km/h or more, for power, speed, and heart rate. Stops do not enter either half. Each half needs at least samples. The two halves must stay within 15 percent for power or for speed. If they do not, the number is a pace change, not drift. Decoupling is (first half minus second half) divided by the first half. A positive number means the second half needs more beats for the same output. The lines at ± percent are his limits (C2). A filled point is a steady run (C3).

    One run, minute by minute

    The heart-rate shade is the Apple limit for a 5-second sample. For running in all conditions, 88 percent of samples are within 5 bpm of a chest strap. 94 percent are within 10 bpm. The pace shade is about ±5 percent from outdoor GPS tests. An indoor run has no pace shade. Power has no shade.

    Durability

    This chart is decoupling against duration. Couzens uses it on steady easy runs, not on intervals (C3). A long steady run inside ±5 percent is a candidate for your aerobic heart rate (C6). This file has no lactate test, so that heart rate is an estimate.

    Best efforts

    These curves are the best mean power and the best mean speed over each window, from Apple Watch samples. Samples above km/h or W are removed as sensor faults (M4). Sprint reps stay in. Each window must be mostly continuous. Run–walk intervals stay out of these curves (C3). Do not compare these watts with Stryd or Garmin.

    Fatigue curve

    Couzens fits best power against duration. The slope is the fatigue index. When duration doubles, power falls by 1 − 2 to that power. He also describes that fall as about two thirds of the index, so an index of −0.10 is a fall of about 6.7% (Fatigue Curves, 2015; calculator, 2013). A fall under 8% is what he asks before an athlete holds an easy aerobic effort for about 10 hours. A very flat long-course curve falls about 5–6%. The value at 1 hour on the line is his FTP-style estimate. It is the line, not a 60-minute test (C19).

    A lactate test can stand in for the long efforts. He finds the lactate curve falls about twice as fast as this field curve (2012). This export has no lactate. The points are the best mean power and speed in the watch samples. Mean power stands in for normalized power. A run–walk is not in these best efforts.

    An earlier post uses the same name for how tiredness clears (The Fatigue Curve, 2009). Phase 0 clears inside the session. That is the rest in an interval. Phase 1 is fuel, about 1 to 4 days. That is a hard day, then an easy day. Phase 2 is muscle repair. A loading block needs a break of about 7 to 14 days for it. Phase 3 is the stress system. It needs a break of months (C20). Decoupling on one run measures drift inside that run (C2). The slope below measures how best power falls as the effort gets longer.

    Volume and work

    The hours include every workout: runs, rides, walks, and strength. Couzens treats monthly volume as the base dose (C8). The calorie chart is walk and run calories only. When the watch is calibrated, Apple reports 0 ± 9 percent against a lab cart. Other studies often find a calorie error above 10 percent.

    Running economy companions

    These charts show ground contact time, vertical oscillation, and stride length against pace. They do not replace power. They show if a slower pace has a different stride. Faster pace is on the left.

    Heat

    Heat and fluid loss raise heart rate at the same output (C5). His Kona crew gave up about 20 percent of run EF to the conditions. Temperature comes from the workout weather value, converted from Fahrenheit. The export stores humidity as centi-percent. This page corrects that. When the steady filter is on, the points are steady runs.

    Recovery

    Couzens treats recovery as part of the training dose (C18). He keeps volume and intensity as two loads (C7) and reads sleep, resting heart rate, and HRV against your own baseline (C17). A night is the sleep that ends on the morning of the run. A nap from 11:00 to 18:00 is not part of the night (M3).

    The sleep chart and the load charts use every day. They do not use the run filter. The four scatter charts use the duration filter. Green points are the easy band.

    Stated error and independent error

    These two error sets stay separate. Apple’s numbers are from the November 2024 heart-rate and calorie paper. They use samples that the watch agrees to publish. The watch already removes poor samples. The other numbers are from other studies. They are not a calibration of this watch.

    Heart rate during workouts

    Apple compares 5-second readings with a chest strap. Availability is the part of the time that has a published reading.

    Sources, limits, and process

    Same input, same page
    The page depends only on the export zip, config.json, and journal.json (M5). The "as of" time is the newest record in the export. Each sync writes metrics/weeks.csv and metrics/summary.json and commits them to git, so every change in a number has a date and a reason.
    One session, one record
    A copy of a Watch workout from another app, such as Nike Run Club or Sworkit, is removed when it overlaps the Watch workout by half or more. Exact duplicates are removed (M1).
    Run–walk steps
    A Watch run whose steps repeat 4 min / 1 min, 9 min / 1 min, or 12 min / 3 min, at least three times, is a run–walk (M6). It is not scored as a steady run (C3). Recovery on a walk is the run step’s average heart rate minus the lowest heart rate on that walk.
    Fatigue curve
    Best mean power and best mean speed at each duration come from the watch samples (M4). The line is output = anchor × hours ^ index. The anchor is the value at 1 hour. The percent fall when duration doubles is 1 − 2^index (C19). The four recovery phases are a different use of the same name (C20). This export has no lactate test.
    After the races
    The plan end date is the start of about 60 days with no specific 5K work (C21). The first 4 weeks keep 10–40% of the in-season run week. The weeks after that keep about half, through the end of the 60 days. In-season hours are the full weeks of this plan. Easy walks can stay in the week. They are general movement.
    Aerobic threshold
    Couzens finds it from the first rise in lactate. This export has no lactate. A steady run with decoupling inside ±5 percent is only a candidate heart rate (C6).
    Readiness
    The day starts from the SDNN quartile (C16). A resting heart rate 5% over its 28-day median, a night under the sleep target, a known-pace heart rate 5% off normal (C15), or a journal score of 2 or less each lower the day by one level. Two flags give a recovery day at most (C17).
    Process
    Export from the Health app on the phone. Put the zip in this folder, or replace the export.zip link. Double-click "Health Report.command". It starts the service and opens the page. The service watches the folder and rebuilds when a newer export appears. The Sync button forces a check.
